03 · Critical upload issue

The backend was slow.
The experience didn’t have
to feel broken.

I redesigned the waiting experience so progress was visible, the status stayed understandable and users no longer felt compelled to cancel and retry.

20% → 2% upload cancellations

Released in one sprint

A small interface change.
Immediate business impact.

Before · 20% cancel
the upload

A registry could sit here up to 10 minutes.
On average people canceled after 4 — a static “Uploading” card and a lonely spinner told them nothing

After · 2% cancel
the upload

I moved the spinner into the inactive checkbox on the closed card, added clear payment-by-payment progress, and renamed the status — so people could see it working and waited it out.

Also added upload start and completion notifications, with a foundation for future upload reports showing payment errors

I explored moving the block with payment registries being uploaded outside the table. However, this approach would have required significant development effort, and at that point, we weren’t confident it was the best solution.

Later, I tested this concept in UX research and confirmed that the quick improvement we had already implemented was clearer and preferred by users.

04 · Discovery

2 prototypes. 12 interviews.
3 segments.

Sole proprietors, SMBs and enterprise clients helped us separate must-have workflows from tempting but low-value functionality.

Prototype 01
Expose everything

A maximum set of filters, rich registry metadata and sorting options to learn what people actually needed

Purpose: Maximize feature exposure to understand what users actually need and use

  • Maximum set of filters and search
  • Rich registry card with registry ID, sender company, priority, creation time, and other key details
  • Registry count and sorting options

This wasn’t intended to be a final solution. The concept intentionally exposed as much functionality as possible to validate user needs, priorities, and usage patterns during UX research.

Prototype 02
Test the future system

A scalable details model with advanced filtering, new management tools and Alfa-Bank’s future visual language.

Purpose: Validate new product capabilities and assess user readiness for the bank-wide redesign

  • Create registries from existing payments, without file import
  • Add registries to Favorites
  • “How to use” onboarding
  • Export registries to Excel
  • Test user response to the new visual language

Alongside feature validation, the prototype tested how users would respond to the bank’s upcoming visual redesign. This was critical because users had only recently migrated from the legacy platform, and another major interface change could create additional friction

09 · What I’d do differently

Research captures today.
B2B design must also anticipate tomorrow.

Research participants consistently said registry IDs were unnecessary, so we removed them to keep the interface simple. Less than a year later, scale and enterprise support made them essential.

Today I would balance validated feedback with operational complexity, support workflows and long-term product needs — even when those needs are not yet visible to customers.
