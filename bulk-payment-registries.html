Situation

One workspace for a sole trader and a corporation

Payment Registries lets a business work with a group of payments — each registry holds from 1 to 30,000 payments that you can sign, execute or edit together, and check the status of the whole registry and every payment inside it.

I joined at the MVP stage and led product design through launch and large-scale expansion. In 2023 we hit a turning point: as the user base grew, the team understood we had to expand the product — but the real challenge was defining the right set of features.

We wanted one unified workspace for every segment — from small entrepreneurs to large multi-company clients — without making it so complex that small businesses would struggle.

Urgent task — file import

Making people wait, without making them faster

Users started an upload but didn’t wait for it to finish — they canceled and tried again, piling more load onto an already slow system.

Problem

Abandoned uploads

People began an upload and quit before it completed, then re-uploaded the same registry — multiplying the load.

Cause

Speed we couldn’t fix

File processing slowed sharply as the user base grew, and the backend couldn’t be sped up at that moment.

Constraint

Design-only

So the fix had to live entirely in the interface: reduce stress and motivate people to wait it out.

Before · 20% cancel the upload

A registry could sit here up to 10 minutes. On average people canceled after 4 — a static “Uploading” card and a lonely spinner told them nothing.

After · 2% cancel the upload

I moved the spinner into the inactive checkbox on the closed card, added clear payment-by-payment progress, and renamed the status — so people could see it working and waited it out.

20% 2%canceled uploads

A 10× drop in cancellations — no backend changes, just honest states and calm progress. The quick win that funded the bigger bet.

Action — discovery

Two prototypes, twelve honest conversations

After analysing the current state, I built two prototypes to answer one question: how much functionality is enough?

Prototype #1 — maximal

Everything from the legacy system plus features I thought could help, all visible on the main page — to discuss what people actually need.

Prototype #2 — hidden

The same rich feature set, but most settings tucked under buttons — to see how intuitively people would find what they needed.

12 interviews

Legacy users from large business and small-business users of the new bank — plus a few from another bank running the same kind of product.
